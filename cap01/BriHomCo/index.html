Legal Lens for Chapter 1, Section 05: Administrative Agencies and the Regulatory Environment

Every sentence on that page was true. Was the page honest?

Read the Brightwell offer again. The refill really is free, shipping really is free, and the recurring charge really is disclosed. No single statement is false. Yet the large “FREE” headline, the $0.00 total and the tiny gray footnote work together to suggest something the fine print contradicts: that joining costs nothing.

Under the Federal Trade Commission Act, the Federal Trade Commission evaluates the net impression a marketing message creates for a reasonable consumer, not each sentence in isolation. A prominent free offer combined with a recurring charge hidden in fine print may be deceptive even when every statement is literally accurate.

Which source of law governs this page?

The same hierarchy you studied in this chapter applies, but two levels do most of the work here.

  1. ConstitutionGrants Congress power over interstate commerce. Brightwell sells across state lines, so federal rules can reach this offer.
  2. StatuteThe FTC Act prohibits unfair or deceptive acts or practices in commerce. It sets the standard, but does not list every misleading layout.
  3. AgencyThe FTC gives that standard concrete meaning: it can investigate campaigns, issue rules within its authority and bring enforcement actions.
  4. CourtsReview agency action and interpret the statute, so the meaning of “deceptive” keeps developing case by case.

The highlighted levels are the ones a Brightwell manager would consult first.

Beyond compliance

Suppose Brightwell’s lawyers could defend the page. Would the customers who discover a $14.99 charge on their bank statements feel they were treated fairly? Management ethics scholarship warns that conduct which is formally lawful but perceived as opportunistic erodes trust, and that organizations which behave ethically only when observed develop fragile compliance cultures (Treviño & Nelson, 2021).

Think it through. If you were Brightwell’s marketing manager, what single change to the page would make its net impression match its fine print? Would that change cost the company customers, and is that a reason not to make it?
