How many sources of law govern one bottle?

Brightwell Home Co. makes this all-purpose cleaner in State A and sells it in States B and C. It carries a label, a marketing claim, a QR code to a refill subscription, a safety seal and a child-resistant trigger.

    The layers stack on the same object

    In X-ray view, each shell is one level of the hierarchy you studied: the Constitution outermost, then statutes, agency regulations and, closest to the product, the common law built by courts. Choose a layer to see which parts it reaches.

    What to notice. Every part sits inside the constitutional shell, and almost every part is governed by two layers at once. Identifying “the” applicable law usually means identifying several, and knowing which one prevails when they collide.

    From the bottle to the method

    Section 06 describes how a manager identifies the applicable law. Here is the method applied to the recycling instructions:

    1. Characterize the situation. The same bottle carries different recycling instructions for State B and State C.
    2. Identify the legal issue. Which disposal and labeling duties apply to a product sold in two states?
    3. Determine the jurisdiction. Recycling is largely regulated by each state, within limits set by any applicable federal law.
    4. Locate the level of authority. State statutes, detailed by state agency rules; federal law prevails only where it validly preempts them.
    5. Anticipate interpretation. Would a court read the federal labeling rules as leaving room for state recycling instructions? The chapter suggests asking how courts and agencies are likely to read the rule, not how Brightwell would like them to.

    Now choose a different part of the bottle and run the same five steps. Note where you were unsure.
